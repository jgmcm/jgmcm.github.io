01 / Themes

Research

How atomic-scale structure sets the electronic properties of two-dimensional materials.

I use theory, first-principles calculations, atomistic simulation and machine learning to connect the structure of layered materials to their electronic properties.

My work combines published studies with developing directions, in close collaboration with electron-microscopy and transport groups at the National Graphene Institute and elsewhere. See Recruiting for opportunities to get involved.

01 · Developing direction

Machine learning for materials

I am developing machine-learned interatomic potentials for layered materials and van der Waals heterostructures, focusing on the weak interlayer interactions that control stacking, sliding and reconstruction. Trained on first-principles data, these models enable large-scale simulations near DFT accuracy. I also explore models that predict electronic properties from structure and neural quantum states for correlated electrons.

02 · Moiré and ferroic materials

Moiré materials and ferroelectricity

I model twisted bilayers across the full range of angles, from small-angle lattice reconstruction in transition-metal dichalcogenides and NbSe₂ to large-angle heterostructures and the 30° quasicrystalline limit. At small angles, stacking domains and domain-wall networks reshape the electronic potential and can form self-organised quantum dots; at larger angles, lattice matching and interlayer hybridisation become central.

In sliding ferroelectrics, stacking controls polarisation and the electronic states at domain walls and twin boundaries. I study these states, including two-dimensional electron gases, compare ferroelectric properties across van der Waals chalcogenides, and extend this work to sliding ferroic multipoles in antiferromagnets.

03 · Electronic structure

Electronic structure of 2D semiconductors

I refine first-principles methods for band-edge parameters in 2D semiconductors, including effective masses, spin–orbit splittings, valley energies and gate response. Recent work uses measured spin–orbit splitting to calibrate DFT for monolayer MoS₂, explains gate-tunable band edges in few-layer MoS₂, and applies extended Hubbard corrections to band-edge orbital character in MoS₂, MoSe₂, WS₂ and WSe₂. These studies connect modelling with quantum-transport measurements.

04 · Defects and interfaces

Defects, curvature and interfaces

I model dislocations and ripplocations in graphite and bilayer graphene, and strain-induced dislocation networks in hBN. I study how defects and sub-nanometre curvature redistribute charge and alter electronic response, including flexoelectricity in graphene, alongside diffusion and friction in layered materials.

With experimental collaborators, I also use atomistic modelling and DFT to study gold species at liquid–solid interfaces, transition-metal diiodides and lithium intercalation in bilayer graphene.
